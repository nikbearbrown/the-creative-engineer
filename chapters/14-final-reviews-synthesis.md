## The result

Submit the final experience and portfolio case study with a traceable account of framing, design, conducting, evaluation, and revision. Complete the second presentation group using the same standards as the first.

The final claim is not that AI eliminated engineering work. The claim you must support is that you made and defended consequential design decisions, conducted bounded AI work, inspected the result, and improved the experience using evidence.

## Apply the same review standard

Group 2 demonstrates the critical journey, a consequential failure path, and the evidence behind selected decisions. Use the Chapter 13 review ledger and the same response dispositions.

Group 1 may continue its documented revisions while Group 2 presents, but both groups have equal requirements and the same final revision deadline. Canvas supplies dates and logistics.

Do not create a new feature sprint because the semester is ending. Complete the required experience, repair consequential defects, and make the record accurate.

## Audit the chain from intention to outcome

Use one row per material claim:

| Link | Audit question |
|---|---|
| Framing | Which audience task justified the work? |
| Design | Which alternative was selected, and why? |
| Conducting | What context and authority did Claude receive? |
| Implementation | Which candidate contains the resulting change? |
| Evaluation | What observation or check supports the claim? |
| Revision | What changed after review, and was it rechecked? |
| Portfolio | Does the public-facing account match that evidence? |

These links need not all be separate documents. They do need stable references and consistent meanings.

The problem-formulation source preserves original and revised briefs. The critique source preserves response decisions. The portfolio source preserves contribution evidence. Use those practices to make change intelligible without rewriting the original history. [Sources C01, C03, and B04](../research/sources.md).

## Ask Claude to find gaps, not invent completion

Provide the final index and ask:

~~~text
Audit this evidence chain.
For each material claim, identify its supporting artifacts and version.
Report missing links, contradictions, unsupported outcomes,
and unclear human/AI contributions.
Classify each gap as needing a file, a test, a human decision,
or an explicit limitation.
Do not fill gaps with a plausible story.
~~~

Inspect the findings yourself. A missing link might be a genuinely absent check or merely a badly named file. Resolve the distinction before requesting another implementation.

Do not let the final case study describe a newer candidate than the submitted artifact. Freeze the submission reference, then make the narrative point to it.

## Worked example: the objective changed

Suppose the project began as an automatic-answer tool and ended as resource discovery with an explicit no-source state.

An inaccurate case study describes the original ambition as if it shipped. A useful case study explains the change:

1. The initial brief proposed automatic answers.
2. The available evidence supported evaluating resource references more directly.
3. The selected design narrowed the journey to discovery and inspection.
4. Claude implemented within that narrower contract.
5. The final record identifies the checks performed and questions still open.

This hypothetical account demonstrates a scope revision. It does not establish that the change improved learning or that every student should make the same choice.

For a personal portfolio, the equivalent revision might move from listing technologies to presenting problem-and-evidence case studies. Explain the audience decision that motivated it and the evidence you actually collected.

## Separate three kinds of unfinished work

Use clear categories:

- **Defect:** the candidate violates an accepted requirement.
- **Accepted trade-off:** the design deliberately favors one objective with a stated cost.
- **Missing evidence:** a claim has not been established.

Each category implies a different response. Repair the defect, defend or revise the trade-off, and obtain evidence or narrow the claim. Do not apply one “future work” label to all three.

The values-and-judgment source separates calculating consequences from choosing objectives. At the end of the project, the human still owns the reasons for those objectives. [Source H02](../research/sources.md).

## Transfer the method

Choose a new domain without implementing a second project. Write a one-page transfer brief:

~~~text
New audience and task:
What remains useful from this project's method:
What assumptions no longer hold:
New authority or data boundaries:
First bounded experiment:
Evidence that would change the design:
~~~

A portfolio, public-interest service, learning tool, and original AI product can share the conducting method without sharing identical interfaces or risk boundaries. Transfer the questions, not an unquestioned template.

Return to Ideate, Build, Brand, Ship. For each verb, identify one decision you now make more explicitly and one check you still need to improve. Use an artifact to support the reflection. Do not substitute an inspirational statement for an account of practice.

## Final packaging

Confirm that the entry point opens, the selected candidate is identifiable, evidence links resolve for the intended reviewer, and limitations are visible. Keep private material out of public artifacts. A local submission and a public portfolio may need different evidence packaging.

## Practice

1. Audit one final claim through every link.
2. Locate a mismatch between narrative and candidate version.
3. Explain a changed objective without rewriting history.
4. Separate a defect from a trade-off.
5. Identify a claim with missing evidence.
6. Ask Claude to classify gaps and audit its classifications.
7. Repair one broken evidence reference.
8. Narrow an unsupported outcome claim.
9. State one consequential human decision.
10. Identify what does not transfer to a new domain.
11. Propose a first bounded experiment for another product.
12. Complete the four-verb self-audit using project artifacts.

## Submission checkpoint — A6

Submit the final experience and portfolio case study, including the reviewed candidate, design references, contribution record, evaluation evidence, critique responses, revisions, and limitations. Complete Group 2 presentations and the shared final revision process.

Weekly studio artifacts feed the six graded submissions. They do not create eleven separate implementation assignments. The course ends with an inspectable design-and-conducting practice, not a claim that every generated artifact is finished.
